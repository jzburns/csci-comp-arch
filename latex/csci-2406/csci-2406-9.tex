\begin{frame}{From Assembly to Machine Code}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
  \item A processor repeatedly \key{fetches}, \key{decodes}, and \key{executes} instructions.
  \item During fetch--decode--execute, the processor fetches a 32-bit instruction word from memory and interprets its bit fields.
  \item This topic starts with the earlier step: how an AArch64 assembly instruction is encoded as that fixed \key{32-bit} machine instruction.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.18cm}

\begin{frame}{AArch64 Encoding Essentials}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{7pt}
\item Every A64 instruction is a 32-bit word containing operation and operand fields.
\item Different instruction families use different field layouts.
\item Register fields are normally 5 bits, encoding register numbers 0--31.
\item There are 31 general-purpose registers. Register number 31 denotes the zero register or stack pointer, depending on the instruction and operand.
\item Identify the instruction form before interpreting its fields.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\sectiondivider{Section 2}{AArch64 Encoding Families}

\begin{frame}{Where Do Opcode Patterns Come From?}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{8pt}
\item \key{Arm assigns the encoding templates.} Look up the fixed family/form bits in the ISA; they cannot be calculated from the mnemonic.
\item \key{Select the form first:} \code{add x5, x6, x7} uses a shifted-register form; \code{add x5, x6, \#24} uses an immediate form with a different template.
\item \key{Fill the selectors:} width, operation, flag update, shift type, access size, or addressing mode choose among related encodings.
\item \key{Insert the operands:} register numbers, immediates, and offsets occupy the remaining fields.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.15cm}
\begin{block}{A Prefix Is Not Necessarily One Opcode Field}
Leading bits may combine fixed family bits with selectors. Each table states the form and bit positions to which its patterns apply.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}

\begin{frame}{Common Data-Processing Register Patterns}
\small
All rows: \key{64-bit shifted-register forms}, \code{sf=1}, \code{shift=00} (LSL). Shift amount: \code{imm6}.
\vspace{0.15cm}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{Arithmetic: fixed bits 28:24 = \code{01011}}
\par\vspace{6pt}{\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Instruction} & \textbf{op / S} & \textbf{Prefix 31:21} \\
\midrule
\code{add} & \code{0 / 0} & \code{10001011000} \\
\code{adds} & \code{0 / 1} & \code{10101011000} \\
\code{sub} & \code{1 / 0} & \code{11001011000} \\
\code{subs} & \code{1 / 1} & \code{11101011000} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{Logical: fixed bits 28:24 = \code{01010}}
\par\vspace{6pt}{\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Instruction} & \textbf{opc} & \textbf{Prefix 31:21} \\
\midrule
\code{and} & \code{00} & \code{10001010000} \\
\code{orr} & \code{01} & \code{10101010000} \\
\code{eor} & \code{10} & \code{11001010000} \\
\code{ands} & \code{11} & \code{11101010000} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{0.2cm}
\key{Arithmetic prefix:} \code{sf | op | S | 01011 | shift | 0}.\\[0.08cm]
\key{Logical prefix:} \code{sf | opc | 01010 | shift | N}, with \code{N=0} here.
\vspace{0.12cm}

For logical forms, \code{opc} is bits 30:29; \code{N} is bit 21. Setting \code{N=1} inverts the shifted second operand. For 32-bit versions, set \code{sf=0}; shifts must be 0--31.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Common Arithmetic Immediate Patterns}
\small
ADD/SUB (immediate) uses \code{sf | op | S | 100010 | sh | imm12 | Rn | Rd}.
The six fixed bits \code{100010} occupy bits 28:23; \code{sh} is bit 22.
\vspace{0.16cm}
\par\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\par\vspace{3pt}{\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{64-bit form} & \textbf{op} & \textbf{S} & \textbf{Prefix 31:23} \\
\midrule
\code{add Xd, Xn, \#imm}  & \code{0} & \code{0} & \code{100100010} \\
\code{adds Xd, Xn, \#imm} & \code{0} & \code{1} & \code{101100010} \\
\code{sub Xd, Xn, \#imm}  & \code{1} & \code{0} & \code{110100010} \\
\code{subs Xd, Xn, \#imm} & \code{1} & \code{1} & \code{111100010} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}\vspace{3pt}
\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{block}{Derive the ADD Immediate Prefix}
\code{sf=1, op=0, S=0}: \code{1 | 0 | 0 | 100010 = 100100010}.\\
For \code{\#24}, choose \code{sh=0} and encode 24 directly in \code{imm12}. With \code{sh=1}, the immediate is shifted left by 12 bits.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Encoding Example: \texttt{add x5, x6, \#24}}

\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]

\begin{block}{Operands}

Rn &= x6 = 00110 \\
\text{imm12} &= 24 = 000000011000
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{block}

\column{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Instruction Meaning}
\[

\begin{frame}{AArch64 Immediate Encodings Are Specialized}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{7pt}
  \item Different instruction families have different immediate encodings.
  \item Some constants fit directly in an \code{add} or \code{sub} immediate.
  \item Other constants require move-wide instructions such as \code{movz}, \code{movn}, or \code{movk}.
  \item Logical instructions use their own bitmask-immediate encoding.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Immediate Fields}
Do not ask whether a constant fits ``in AArch64'' in general. Ask whether it fits
in the specific immediate field of the instruction being used.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Decoding Checklist}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{8pt}
  \item Convert the hexadecimal word to 32 binary bits.
  \item Identify the broad encoding family from the opcode bits.
  \item Split the word into the fields for that family.
  \item Translate register fields into \code{x} or \code{w} register names.
  \item Interpret immediates carefully: sign extension, scaling, and shifts matter.
  \item Reconstruct the assembly instruction.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Important}
The same bit positions can mean different things in different instruction families.
Always identify the family before naming the fields.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Deriving Load/Store Unsigned-Offset Prefixes}
\small
For integer \key{unsigned-offset} transfers, bits 31:22 are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.85cm,y=1cm]
\bfld{0}{0}{2}{11}{lightblue}
\bfld{2}{0}{3}{111}{gold!22}
\bfld{5}{0}{1}{0}{lightblue}
\bfld{6}{0}{2}{01}{gold!22}
\bfld{8}{0}{2}{01}{lightblue}
\bitnote{1}{0.95}{31:30: size}
\bitnote{3.5}{0.95}{29:27: fixed}
\bitnote{5.5}{0.95}{26: VR}
\bitnote{7}{0.95}{25:24: fixed}
\bitnote{9}{0.95}{23:22: opc}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
\item \code{size}: \code{00}/\code{01}/\code{10}/\code{11} select 1/2/4/8-byte accesses for the transfers in the next table.
\item \code{VR=0}: use general-purpose integer registers, rather than SIMD/FP registers.
\item \code{opc=00}: store; \code{opc=01}: load without sign extension. Sign-extending loads use other \code{opc} values.
\item \code{111} and \code{01} are ISA-assigned fixed bits for this form.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.14cm}
\key{64-bit load:} \code{11 | 111 | 0 | 01 | 01 = 1111100101}.\\
\key{64-bit store:} change \code{opc} to \code{00}, giving \code{1111100100}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Common Memory Instruction Patterns}

\begin{frame}{Addressing Mode Also Selects the Encoding}
\small
The mnemonic \code{ldr} alone does not determine the fixed bits. Compare these \key{64-bit integer load} forms:
\vspace{0.12cm}
\par\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\par\vspace{3pt}{\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Assembly form} & \textbf{Fixed leading bits} & \textbf{Offset / mode fields} \\
\midrule
\code{ldr Xt, [Xn, \#off]} & \code{1111100101} (31:22) & \code{imm12}, scaled by 8 \\
\code{ldr Xt, [Xn, \#off]!} & \code{11111000010} (31:21) & signed \code{imm9}; 11:10 = \code{11} \\
\code{ldr Xt, [Xn], \#off} & \code{11111000010} (31:21) & signed \code{imm9}; 11:10 = \code{01} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}\vspace{3pt}
\vspace{0.06cm}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
\item Unsigned offset: no writeback. Pre-index \code{!}: update the base before access. Post-index: update it after access.
\item In the two writeback forms, \code{imm9} (20:12) is a signed \key{byte} offset, $-256$ to $255$. \code{Rn}/\code{Rt} remain in bits 9:5 / 4:0.
\item For unsigned-offset \code{ldr}, bits 11:10 belong to \code{imm12}; they are not separate mode selectors.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{PC-Relative Addresses for Memory Access}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
  \item PC-relative instructions use the current instruction address as their reference; \code{adrp} uses its 4 KiB page base.
  \item This supports position-independent code and nearby constants or labels.
  \item For \code{adr} and literal loads, PC is the address of the current instruction.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Common PC-Relative Forms}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
  \item \code{adr}: forms an address near the current PC.
  \item \code{adrp}: forms a page address relative to the current PC page.
  \item \code{ldr literal}: loads data from a nearby PC-relative literal location.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\sectiondivider{Section 5}{Branch Instruction Encodings}

\begin{frame}{Common Immediate Branch Patterns}
\small
B/BL use a 6-bit prefix; conditional branches use 8 fixed high bits and a condition field.
\par\vspace{3pt}{\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Instruction} & \textbf{Fixed leading bits} & \textbf{Displacement field} & \textbf{cond (3:0)} \\
\midrule
\code{b label} & \code{000101} (31:26) & \code{imm26} (25:0) & --- \\
\code{bl function} & \code{100101} (31:26) & \code{imm26} (25:0) & --- \\
\code{b.eq label} & \code{01010100} (31:24) & \code{imm19} (23:5) & \code{0000} \\
\code{b.ne label} & \code{01010100} (31:24) & \code{imm19} (23:5) & \code{0001} \\
\code{b.lt label} & \code{01010100} (31:24) & \code{imm19} (23:5) & \code{1011} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par}\vspace{3pt}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
\item \key{B versus BL:} bit 31 selects branch (\code{0}) or branch-with-link (\code{1}); bits 30:26 stay \code{00101}.
\item \key{Conditional branches:} bit 4 is fixed at \code{0}; \code{cond} selects EQ (Z=1), NE (Z=0), or signed LT (N differs from V).
\item \key{All five rows:} target = current instruction address + sign-extended displacement $\times 4$. \code{bl} also writes PC + 4 to \code{x30}.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Unconditional Branch Encoding}

\begin{block}{Instruction Forms}
\[
\code{b label}
\qquad\qquad
\code{bl function}
\]

\begin{frame}{Branch Example: Backward Loop}
\small
Assume \code{x0=0} and \code{x1} is a positive iteration count. The branch returns to an earlier instruction while \code{x1} is nonzero.

\begin{frame}{Common Encoding Mistakes}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{7pt}
  \item Treating a register number as decimal text instead of a binary field.
  \item Forgetting that AArch64 register fields are usually 5 bits.
  \item Encoding a byte offset directly when the immediate field is scaled.
  \item Adding a pipeline offset to PC instead of using the branch instruction address.
  \item Reading little-endian memory bytes as if they were the printed instruction word.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}

\begin{block}{Practical Habit}
Write the bit ranges first, then fill in the fields. Do not try to guess the final hexadecimal word too early.
\end{block}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Topic 7: Summary}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{7pt}
  \item AArch64 instructions are fixed-width 32-bit words.
  \item The meaning of each bit field depends on the instruction family.
  \item Fixed family patterns are looked up in the ISA; selector fields explain the related instruction variants.
  \item Data-processing immediate instructions use a different field layout.
  \item Load/store instructions commonly encode a base register, target/source register, and scaled offset.
  \item Direct immediate branches encode PC-relative offsets scaled by 4 bytes; register-indirect branches use a register target.
  \item In \code{b.cond}, bits 3:0 encode the condition and \code{imm19} encodes the signed instruction displacement.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}
